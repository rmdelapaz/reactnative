# Final Project Rubric

React Native & Expo: Build and Ship a Mobile App, Lesson 10.1. The same rubric is used for
self-assessment, peer review and (in a class) the instructor's review. Each row scores 0 (not yet),
1 (meets) or 2 (strong), and maps to the modules that taught it. The project is **complete** when no
row is "not yet"; "strong" rows are what make it a portfolio piece. "Not yet" is not a failing grade:
it is the list of what to do next.

**Nothing in this rubric needs money.** No row needs an Apple Developer Program membership, a Google
Play account, a paid Expo plan or a paid server. Where a row mentions store accounts, it is one way to
reach "strong", never the only one.

| # | Criterion (modules) | Not yet (0) | Meets (1) | Strong (2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plan** (10; every module's "your own app" notes) | Worksheet sections missing, or copied from Shoreline Crew without thought | Every section filled for this brief; decisions have reasons; milestones and time log kept | Trade-offs argued with evidence (local vs server data, Expo Go vs development build, what is out of scope and why); estimate vs actual hours reflected on |
| 2 | **Screens and navigation** (2, 4) | A screen that is blank, cut off or unreachable; a dead end with no way back; text under the notch or the home indicator | Every screen in the worksheet's route plan works; safe areas respected; light and dark both readable; a not-found screen; at least one screen opens from a deep link | Tabs, stacks and modals chosen per the worksheet's reasons; a wide screen (tablet, a phone on its side, the web preview) uses the space; linked screens have something sensible underneath |
| 3 | **Data and server state** (1.3, 3, 5.1, 5.2) | `any` or `as` to silence errors; API data used before it is checked; server data copied into `useState`; lists that do not update after a change | Domain types in `types.ts` with unions and complete `Record` tables; every answer checked; TanStack Query factories and mutations that invalidate the right keys; loading, error (Try again), empty and not-found states in words people understand; `npx tsc --noEmit` clean | Computed values never stored; long lists measured and fast (FlashList or paging where the worksheet planned it); refetch on returning to the app and on reconnecting works on a phone |
| 4 | **Storage and offline** (5.3, 5.5) | A token or secret in AsyncStorage or in the code; data lost on a restart that the plan said to keep; the app breaks or spins forever without a connection | Every piece of kept data in the store the worksheet chose (SecureStore for secrets only); what the app does offline is written down and works: a queue with ids made on the phone, or a clear "you are offline" with the last answer, or local-only data | Offline changes tested (a resend creates no duplicate); the person can see what has not been sent yet; sign-out clears what it should |
| 5 | **Forms and input** (2.4, 5.4) | Fields without labels; errors only in the console; the keyboard covers the field being typed in | One schema per form for its rules and types; the right keyboard, return keys that move to the next field; errors under their fields, the server's own rules included; nothing hidden by the keyboard | Thumb controls where they beat typing (steppers, choices, switches); the form survives a slow or failed send without losing what was typed |
| 6 | **Device features** (6) | A permission asked at launch, with no reason, or a "no" that breaks the screen | Each device feature in the brief works on a phone; permissions asked in context with a purpose string that says why; every "no" leaves a way forward; the web preview shows a stand-in, not a crash | A feature dropped or replaced because it was not needed (a picker instead of the camera); behavior after "no", "not now" and "blocked" checked on a real phone and written up |
| 7 | **Accessibility and motion** (7, 8.1) | Controls a screen reader cannot name; targets smaller than 44 points; text that cannot grow; motion that ignores Reduce Motion | Labels and roles on every control; targets at least 44 x 44 (48 on Android); the largest text size checked on every screen; Reduce Motion respected; axe clean on the web preview | A full VoiceOver or TalkBack pass on a real phone, written up with what you fixed; motion that explains a change, not decoration |
| 8 | **Tests, CI and performance** (8.2-8.4) | No tests, or tests that fail; lint warnings ignored | Tests for the mock API's rules, the main logic and at least two screens with React Native Testing Library; `npx expo lint` clean; CI green on the latest commit; one performance measurement written down | The "must not happen" test from the worksheet; a regression test for a bug you really had; a measurement that led to a change |
| 9 | **Config and release** (9.1-9.4) | A secret in the repository or in an `EXPO_PUBLIC_` variable; ids still `com.example.starter`; no idea what the app collects | Development, preview and production variants with your own names and ids; `eas.json` profiles that match them (with tests); a data inventory and draft App Privacy and Data safety answers; the release checklist walked, every line marked done, not possible yet, or to do | Updates vs store releases decided per kind of change (9.3); a privacy policy draft and, if the app creates accounts, a deletion path; the release checklist turned into the README's "how to release" |
| 10 | **Launch, review and reflection** (9.2, 10) | Nobody but you has seen it run; no peer review given or received | **One of these, all free:** a preview build installed from an EAS internal distribution link (a free Expo account; Android, or iPhone where you have the membership), OR a recorded demo of the main journey in Expo Go or a development build on a real phone, OR, only for an app without device features, a recorded demo in the web preview. Plus a README with how to run it, and a peer review given and received, every point answered | Two testers used it on their own phones and you acted on what they said; or (only if you already have the accounts) a build on Google Play internal testing or TestFlight; a written reflection on what you would do differently |
